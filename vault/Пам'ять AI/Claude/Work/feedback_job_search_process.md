Artur applies to very few companies per week, chosen deliberately, and goes
deep on each rather than mass-applying ("не вісімдесят відгуків за суботу, а
кілька компаній, які розбираю дуже детально"). He has already gotten offers
this way and has had a rejecting recruiter come back with a new opening
later — more than once.

**The process, in order:**

1. **Company screen first, before touching the CV.** Read what the company
   does, who they sell to, recent news (funding, leadership changes,
   direction shifts, or notable silence). If interest drops after ~10
   minutes of this, he closes the tab and writes nothing — no resume, no
   letter, no application.
2. **Tailored CV + short cover letter**, only for companies that pass step 1.
   CV trimmed to what the posting actually asked for. Cover letter has
   exactly three things: why this company, something similar he's already
   done, what he can verify/prove in the first weeks. No "I've always dreamed
   of working here" filler. (See [[feedback-job-search-voice]] for the tone
   rules on these documents.)
3. **LinkedIn recon in parallel.** Look up who posted the role, who the
   likely manager is, who already holds a similar title there. Send a short
   note to a few of them: what he looked at, why he's reaching out, one real
   question.
4. **Visibility while waiting on the application.** Read their feed, leave a
   genuine comment (not empty praise) on a company or team-member post — so
   his name has already surfaced once before any interview.
5. **Pre-interview re-prep.** Before a call, re-read the company, the
   product, and the hiring manager's own public writing. He specifically
   checks whether the job post language and the manager's own posts
   disagree — better to catch that mismatch before the call than during it.
6. **Post-interview self-review, same day.** Right after, he writes down
   where he answered weakly or over-talked, and fixes it before the next
   conversation while it's still fresh.
7. **No "any update?" follow-ups.** He does not ping the recruiter the next
   day asking status — it doesn't speed up a decision, it just adds to an
   already full inbox.
8. **Never write off a recruiter after a rejection.** They go into a
   tracking table instead. Roughly once a month, if that recruiter posts
   something relevant, he leaves a normal on-topic comment — no "got
   anything open?" ask, just staying visible. He says the next role from
   that same recruiter tends to surface this way more often than a single
   rejection letter would suggest, and it has already happened to him
   several times.

9. **Always verify the attached CV matches the role before sending.** Don't
   assume the last-used CV file is the right one. When a role calls for a
   tailored CV, generate/save the tailored version per company, download it
   locally, and attach that specific file to the application form rather than
   whatever was last on hand.

**Why:** This is Artur's own description of how he actually searches — the
guardrail is that shallow, high-volume applying is explicitly the thing he's
avoiding. Step 9 came from him flagging a case where the wrong CV almost went
out — the fix is to make CV-per-company + explicit verification a standing
step, not a one-off catch.

**How to apply:** When helping with anything in
`C:\Users\derda\Work\job-search\`, default to this shape: screen the company
before drafting anything, keep CVs/letters tailored per step 2, suggest the
LinkedIn touchpoints in steps 3-4 as part of the workflow (not as an
afterthought), prompt for a same-day post-interview note (step 6), never
suggest a "checking in" follow-up email (step 7), and treat a rejected
recruiter as a long-term contact to track and occasionally re-engage (step 8)
rather than a closed lead.

**How to apply (step 9 detail):** For every application, confirm which CV
version is being uploaded before submitting, and default to producing a
per-company tailored CV (via `cv-tailor`), saving it locally, and attaching
that exact file rather than reusing a generic or previous company's version.

10. **Never use a platform's formal "Decline" action on an inbound recruiter
    message, even when the role is obviously a bad fit** (e.g. the posting
    itself says his background won't work). He corrected this live on
    2026-09-11 after watching a Djinni "Decline" go out on a mismatched
    Loona Agency proposal: "ніколи не відмовляйся... навіть якщо дуже не
    підходжу." Declining formally closes the thread; he wants to stay in the
    conversation instead. When a role's own listed requirements rule him out,
    say so honestly in a normal reply (or an anonymous reply, if the platform
    offers one, to avoid committing contact info he may not want to share
    yet) — never hit the platform's Decline/reject button. This is the same
    instinct as step 8: don't self-select out of a recruiter relationship
    just because one specific role doesn't fit.

11. **Never lead with what he lacks or doesn't fit — lead with strengths,
    always.** Corrected live on 2026-09-11 right after step 10, when a reply
    to a recruiter opened with "I don't have hands-on marketing experience,
    so I probably don't fit": "не вказуй що я не підходжу, завжди тільки
    плюси підкреслюй, мені ж роботу знайти треба!" Even when a posting's
    stated requirements don't line up with his background, don't write a
    self-disqualifying reply. Reframe: pull out the closest real experience
    that maps to what they're asking (e.g. Otake's growth work — acquisition
    funnels, campaigns, partnerships, metrics — as marketing-adjacent proof
    even though it's not classic SEO/PPC/SMM), state it with confidence, and
    invite the next step. Still never invent experience he doesn't have —
    reframe true facts positively, don't fabricate new ones.
