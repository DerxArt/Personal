Do not write Threads comments as clean "observation → witty twist" epigrams. The first batch of 10 comment drafts for [[threads-content-strategy]] all shared one shape: setup sentence, then a polished aphoristic punchline, heavy on em dashes and "не X, а Y" negative-parallelism constructions ("Мовчання клієнта — це окремий вид тиші, голосніший за «дорого»"). The user rejected all 10 as "AI слов" on sight.

Fix applied: ran the anthropic-skills:humanizer skill over the batch. Concretely this meant, per comment: drop the em-dash-driven twist structure, drop negative parallelism ("не шукати, а розморожувати"), drop inflated superlatives ("найнедооціненіша межа", "найчесніша стратегія яку бачив"), and instead write a real specific personal reaction — sometimes mild disagreement, sometimes a concrete claim ("я вже двічі купував щось із коментарів під такими постами"), varied sentence length and rhythm, first-person voice. The rewritten batch was approved and published.

**Why:** The user's actual strategic goal for these comments is not to get the original poster's attention — it's for the comment itself to be more shareable/quotable than the post it's under. A comment that sounds machine-generated can't do that; it reads as noise. The user also explicitly said: bring a new thought or antithesis to the post, don't just paraphrase or agree with dressed-up language — that's what makes a comment worth noticing on its own.

**How to apply:** Before presenting any drafted Threads comment (or batch of them) to the user, self-check against the humanizer skill's checklist — especially: no em-dash epigram twist, no "не X, а Y", no inflated/superlative framing, no uniform rhetorical shape repeated across multiple drafts in the same batch. Prefer a genuine reaction (agreement with a specific detail, mild pushback, a concrete personal-sounding claim) over a manufactured "wise" one-liner. This applies to all future Threads comment drafting for this account, not just the batch that triggered the correction. See [[threads-content-strategy]] for where this is also recorded operationally in `00-playbook.md`.

**Second occurrence (same session, own posts this time):** Drafting 3 posts for the next day, the same failure mode showed up in the account's own post copy, not just comments — e.g. "Найпродуктивніший спосіб не відповідати клієнту — вийти на пробіжку. Технічно ти зайнятий здоров'ям, а не ігнором." The tell was the same: a hedge word ("технічно") or tacked-on comparison clause that explains why the observation is ironic, instead of just stating two flat facts and trusting the reader to see the irony. The user's own proven top post ("Work-life balance: закрив ноутбук, відкрив Strava.") never explains itself — it's two blunt facts side by side. The fix that landed: "Клієнт написав «терміново» о 18:00. Я вийшов на пробіжку о 18:05." — concrete times, zero explanation, zero hedge words. So this rule isn't comment-specific: it governs all short-form copy for this account, own posts included. Don't add a clause that narrates the joke's mechanism ("технічно...", "там навіть...", "і це ще без...") — end on the flat fact.

**Third occurrence (14.09.2026) — a new failure mode: template-factory.** After Insights showed two post structures had driven the week's best numbers (an identity/threshold question, and a two-flat-facts contrast), the next content-plan draft turned both into literal fill-in-the-blank templates and produced four posts by plugging new variables into the same two sentence skeletons — e.g. reusing "Скільки X, щоб чесно назвати себе Y?" verbatim and "X. Я зробив Y." verbatim with only the nouns swapped. The user's reaction: "всі занадто неживі" (they're all lifeless) and, in their own words, warned against exactly this — "не робити прям шаблон-фабрику, де ми випускаємо ці самі пости, надіючись що вони залетять" (don't turn this into a template factory cranking out the same posts hoping they'll take off).

**Why this matters beyond the specific fix:** analyzing *why* a post worked (question invites identification; flat contrast trusts the reader) is useful. Copying its *sentence structure* onto a new topic is not the same thing and produces the same soulless result as the original AI-epigram problem, just at the level of a whole post instead of a phrase. A winning post's value was in being a genuinely specific, freshly-noticed moment — not in its grammatical shape being reusable.

**How to apply (supersedes literal template reuse):** When Insights or past data shows which posts performed best, extract the *principle* (e.g. "specific self-deprecating moments about running get replies," "a flat two-fact juxtaposition gets likes") but write the next post from an actual fresh, specific observation — a real number, a real small moment — not by re-deriving the old post's sentence and swapping nouns. If two or more drafts in the same batch are recognizably the same sentence skeleton, that's the template-factory tell — stop and rewrite from a different starting observation instead of a different filler. Confirmed-working fix on 14.09: drafts built from a concrete, mundane, slightly embarrassing specific detail ("Сьогодні наздогнав людину, яка йшла швидше за мене. Довелося прискоритись, щоб не було соромно.", "Гармін каже, що я пробіг 47 км цього тижня. Ноги кажуть інше.") rather than from the prior winners' grammar.
