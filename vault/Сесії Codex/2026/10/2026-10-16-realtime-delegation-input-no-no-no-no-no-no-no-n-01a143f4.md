## Codex

Absolutely. For these conversations, I’ll give you a short article summary, a few key findings, and any important limits of the research before we discuss it.

Here’s a recent psychology and productivity study, published **9 October 2026**: [Perceived AI support and work outcomes](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1824712/full).

- Researchers surveyed **695 knowledge workers in Germany** who use AI at work.
- When people felt AI supported them, they were more likely to reshape their work proactively—for example, by taking on useful tasks or finding better ways to do them. This was linked with higher job satisfaction, engagement, and *perceived* productivity.
- Another pattern—using AI to avoid or withdraw from demanding parts of work—was linked with lower satisfaction and productivity, and more burnout.
- **Important limit:** the survey captured one point in time, so it shows associations. It can’t prove that AI caused these changes. [Study details and limitations](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1824712/full)

The researchers call proactively adjusting your work **“job crafting.”** Do you think AI helps people shape their work in a better way, or can it make it easier to avoid difficult tasks?

## Codex

Your distinction gets to an important question: **automating the repetitive parts of a task isn’t the same as avoiding responsibility for the result.** In your spreadsheet example, AI might prepare the categories and dashboard, while you check the source data, catch unusual entries, and approve the final version. You remain responsible, but spend less time on manual assembly.

That also sharpens the study’s limits. It surveyed workers about their experiences; it didn’t measure whether AI actually saved them time after review and corrections. So we still need to ask whether the *whole process*—prompting, checking, fixing, and maintaining the system—takes less time and produces reliable work. [Study details](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1824712/full)

How would you decide which tasks AI can **complete**, which it can only **prepare for your review**, and which should stay fully human?
