# English speaking — deeper conversations

Article-informed speaking practice about why people may avoid deeper conversations. The discussion drew on the idea that people can overestimate awkwardness and underestimate connection, while recognizing that the right depth depends on context.

Artur’s view: deeper conversation can signal trust and help people understand one another. With a stranger or in brief networking, starting too deep may feel awkward, so moving gradually from light topics toward more meaningful ones can give both people room to opt in. Artur enjoys deep conversations and takes opportunities to have them. He said the middle step feels more natural, then began to explain what can still make it difficult; the thought was unfinished, so no conclusion is inferred.

## Continued discussion: hobbies and play

The conversation shifted to whether adults should keep hobbies they are not especially good at. Artur said it depends on whether they enjoy the hobby: if they like it, they can keep doing it without turning it into a results-driven project. He described most of his hobbies, including reading and running, as interest-led, while noting that social media comparisons can make running feel competitive.

For Artur, engagement is a cue to move a conversation deeper. He asked to stay with the hobby topic and explained that topic changes should wait until he says he has had enough. Keep the discussion focused and use follow-up questions to explore the current topic rather than repeatedly offering to switch.

Additional language notes: “if they really like the hobby”; “keep doing it”; “hobbies are mostly for enjoyment”; “we should go deeper into a topic.”
