# Otake Tutor Growth Pilot

## Core Bet

We will test a narrow growth motion for adult foreign-language tutors: find tutors who already sell lessons, courses, speaking clubs, or webinars through social content, then create a personalized Otake draft for each one before asking for anything.

The bet is simple: a tutor who currently answers the same booking, price, schedule, and format questions in DMs will care more about a ready draft than about another tool pitch.

## Target Segment

Primary audience: tutors of foreign languages for adults.

Best first subset: English tutors for adults who sell individual lessons, group classes, speaking clubs, exam prep, business English, or IT English.

Why this subset:

- They already have a clear monetization flow.
- Their followers often need price, format, availability, and booking information before buying.
- Many use Instagram, Threads, LinkedIn, and Telegram as their main sales layer.
- Their students are often professionals, freelancers, founders, or people working with international markets, so some students may also become Otake users.

## Lead Criteria

Prioritize profiles with:

- Active content in the last 7-14 days.
- Clear adult-learning angle, not only schoolchildren.
- At least one sales signal: lessons, course, consultation, speaking club, webinar, guide, or waitlist.
- Visible friction in the current flow: "write me in DM", scattered links, pinned posts with prices, multiple highlights, generic Linktree, Telegram-only link, or no clear booking path.
- Content quality good enough that a custom page would make them look more credible.

Avoid profiles with:

- No recent content.
- No obvious monetization.
- Only children-focused tutoring unless the parent journey is very clear.
- Agencies with polished existing funnels.
- Accounts where the offer is unclear even after checking bio, highlights, and pinned posts.

## Segment Rules

Under 20K followers:

- Gift/help only.
- No ask for a story, reel, recommendation, or bio placement in the first concrete offer.
- Goal: get them to review the draft, use it if useful, and trust Otake.

20K-50K followers:

- Default to gift/help first.
- If the draft clearly helps, ask lightly whether they would be open to testing it in bio for a short period.
- Do not frame it like a campaign brief.

50K+ followers:

- Offer two routes: we can simply gift the page and help set it up, or discuss paid collaboration if that is how they prefer to work.
- Ask for rates only after they show interest in paid work.

## 7-Day Test

Day 1:

- Build a list of 50 tutor leads.
- Score each lead from 1 to 5 on fit, current bio friction, and content quality.
- Pick the top 20 for the first manual draft batch.
- Create two reusable Otake page structures: solo tutor and course/speaking-club tutor.

Day 2:

- Create 10 personalized draft pages.
- Warm each lead lightly by liking 1-2 recent posts and saving one specific observation for the DM.
- Send the first opener to 10 leads.

Day 3:

- Create 10 more personalized draft pages.
- Send the first opener to the next 10 leads.
- Reply to anyone who gives permission or asks what this is.

Day 4:

- Follow up only with people who saw the message or interacted.
- If response rate is under 10%, adjust the opener toward the pain: fewer lost students, less repeated DM work, cleaner booking.

Day 5:

- Push the best 2-3 interested leads toward reviewing their draft.
- Ask for one concrete reaction: what feels useful, what feels wrong, what would make them actually use it.

Day 6:

- Check who added the Otake link, clicked the draft, or asked for edits.
- Create 2 short organic posts/Reels from the strongest insight.

Day 7:

- Review numbers.
- Decide whether to continue, change the niche, or narrow further to IT English/business English tutors.

## Success Signals

Strong signal:

- 50 leads researched.
- 20 personalized drafts created.
- 20 openers sent.
- 6+ replies.
- 3+ people view/review the draft.
- 2+ people ask for edits or setup help.
- 1+ person puts the link in bio or shares it with students.

Good enough to iterate:

- 3-5 replies.
- 1-2 draft reviews.
- Clear objections that can be fixed through copy or page structure.

Weak signal:

- Under 2 replies after 20 highly personalized openers.
- No one opens the draft after permission.
- Tutors say the page is useful but not worth changing their current flow.

## Metrics

Track:

- Replies per opener sent.
- Permission-to-send-draft rate.
- Draft link views.
- Draft edit requests.
- Otake link added to bio.
- Actions on the tutor page: booking, payment, materials, contact.
- Students who create their own Otake page after seeing a tutor page.
- Qualitative objections: no time, already have tool, too much effort, no need, cannot change bio link.

## Main Risk

The original candidate idea jumps too quickly into sending the draft link and asking for bio placement. That can feel like pressure, especially in Instagram DMs.

The safer version is:

1. Start with a human observation.
2. Ask permission to explain.
3. Explain that we already made a useful first draft.
4. Offer setup help and zero risk.
5. Only after interest, send the draft link.
6. Only after they like the draft, ask about using it in bio.

## Product Ideas To Test Later

Do not build these before validating the tutor segment. Use them as learning prompts during calls or replies.

Student testimonials:

- Tutor can collect and show short student reviews on the Otake page.
- After a student leaves a review, show a soft CTA to create their own Otake page.

Tutor trust badges:

- Booking count, student rating, or "popular this month" style proof.
- This could encourage tutors to route students through Otake because the page becomes a public proof layer.

Referral path:

- Give tutors a simple referral link after they become active users.
- Position it as a way to help their adult students organize their own professional links, not as a generic affiliate pitch.
